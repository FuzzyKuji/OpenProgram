Provider quota

One status per connected provider: health, quota, 30-day spend, default model and key health. Quota comes first from the replies OpenProgram already receives; an endpoint is read only on a few events, never on a timer.

One model, four kinds

A QuotaLimit is one limit of one account: key, label, unit (percent, usd, tokens, requests), used, limit, remaining, window, reset time, plus its source (headers, frame, endpoint) and fetched_at. A provider's report has one kind:

Status
Warning at 80 % used, danger at 95 %, exhausted at 100 % or nothing remaining. A windowed limit warns early only when usage runs ahead of the time elapsed in its window.
Stale
A value older than 15 minutes is marked stale; it is still shown, with its age.
Account
The stored credential id, or empty for an environment key. A bearer token is matched to its credential by digest; the token itself is never stored.

Reading an endpoint

An endpoint is read only when:

  • the status page opens and the last read of that account is older than 10 minutes, whatever it answered (after answering, in the background). A refused read (401, 5xx, a body that does not parse) counts: otherwise an endpoint that rejects the key would be asked again on every open;
  • the user asks for a refresh;
  • a reply failed with 402, 429 or a quota message (at most once per account per 10 minutes);
  • a key was just added.

One read per account at a time; a 429 from the quota endpoint holds that account for its Retry-After, or 5 minutes. Reads use the configured safe HTTP client, pinned to the endpoint's origin, and are logged as probe kind quota.

Each read is recorded per provider and account in usage.db (quota_reads), and the Worker and every CLI run decide from that record:

  • kept: when it was sent, its HTTP status, why it failed, the 429 hold, the last read a failed reply asked for;
  • so a refused read is not sent again by the next providers status or after a Worker restart;
  • a failed read leaves the earlier values with their own observed time; the status names the failure once below them (read_error, read_at) until a read answers.

Spend

From the usage ledger, per provider over 30 days: calls, cost, list price, tokens by part, cache share (cache reads over prompt tokens) and a daily series of 30 points. A subscription provider reports its spend as Included, with the list price kept beside it. The default model is the last one used, else the most used.

A DeepSeek reply could show more cached tokens than total tokens. The cause: the recorder's fallback wrote total_tokens = input + output, leaving cached tokens out. The recorder now keeps the larger of the reported total and the parts summed, and every aggregate sums the parts, so rows written before the fix read correctly.

usage.db gains quota_snapshots (the latest value per provider, account and limit), quota_samples (one sample per 15 minutes, kept 90 days) and quota_reads (the last endpoint read per provider and account).

Health and failure causes

Two records speak about a key: its credential status, written when a call failed (status_at, the pool's updated_at), and the last read of its quota endpoint (read_at). The newer one decides; at the same time a 401 outranks a stored billing stop or throttle.

  • A 401 read newer than the status: invalid_key ("quota endpoint answered 401"), until a read answers or a call with the key succeeds.
  • A read that answered after a billing_blocked stop overrules the stop; that read's balance decides.

Health is the best key's state, overridden by a spent limit: ready, rate_limited (with the reset time), no_credit, invalid_key, signed_out or unknown.

A throttle lasts as long as its cooldown. The pool keeps a key's rate_limited status until its next success but picks the key again once the cooldown has passed, so health reads such a key as ready, not as rate limited until a time already gone.

The CLI table prints only coming times (until 14:05, resets Nov 19 02:22); a time already passed is left out. It fits the terminal width: a bar gives up cells, down to 5, and then its reset time moves to a line of its own. Output to a pipe without COLUMNS is not wrapped.

A failed reply carries a cause beside its error reason, from the provider error to the turn result, the event (cause) and the stored placeholder (error_cause):

CauseFrom
quota_exhausted402, insufficient_quota, usage_limit_reached; names the spent limit and its reset time when known
invalid_key401 or a rejected key message
not_availableRegion or plan refusal (403 JSON, model not on the plan)
unreachableDNS or connection failure anywhere in the exception chain

An ordinary 429, a 5xx, a timeout and a 403 web page from a network edge carry no cause; the error card is drawn as before.

  • No web UI reads the status route yet.
  • No reader for Kimi Coding (its payload is not settled), DeepSeek or Moonshot balances, or OpenRouter /credits (no peer documents their responses).
  • The unverified readers above are off.
