Center canvas layout

Chinese specification and interactive prototype

Each layout occupies one top-level tab. Its serializable n-ary tree contains row and column splits with normalized weights and pane leaves referencing existing tabs. Existing two-pane groups migrate to row splits with their saved ratio. Content appears once within a layout.

Interaction

The canvas has no persistent layout toolbar. Each pane has a "•••" grip at the top centre with an 88×22px hit area; drag it to move the pane. Under the pointer the grip drops a small grey sheet from the top edge, its shoulders flaring into the edge: it grows down and out from a narrow sliver with a slight spring. The three grey dots are the window buttons themselves at rest; they spread apart and grow one after another into the macOS red, yellow and green buttons, and their glyphs fade in once they are full size. Leaving the grip runs all of it backwards. Red closes the tab itself through the tab strip's own close, which saves open files first; yellow folds the pane back into the tab strip; green opens the tab on its own. Resting on green for 250ms unfolds the tiling menu from that same tab, attached to the grip instead of floating, and it folds away when the pointer leaves the grip. The menu grows in height out of the sheet as the grip widens; when it closes it stays drawn for 200ms to fold back up before the grip narrows and the buttons turn back into dots. Right-click, or ArrowDown on green, opens it pinned. A press on a button that moves 6px becomes a drag and never clicks the button; pointer capture starts only with the drag. Pressing the grip never starts a text selection in the pane. Dividers draw nothing until the pointer rests on the gap, then show a short gray pill in its middle, as between tiled macOS windows; the whole gap stays draggable. A divider stops where a pane would drop below 240×160px; a pane already smaller is never squeezed further. Below 900px the sidebars normally float over the chat, but while the canvas is split the collapsed rails keep their own columns so they never cover a pane.

Drops go through a window overview modelled on Mission Control and the iPad app switcher. When a tab leaves the strip vertically, or a pane's ••• grip starts to move, the center area recedes to the page colour and every window in the strip (a single tab or a split group) becomes a card laid out on a grid sized to show them as large as possible, never wider than half the area. A card keeps its window's split: its panes are drawn from the same layout tree and geometry as the canvas, each showing what it looked like when last on screen. Pictures are kept per tab in memory and in IndexedDB, for one theme only (a theme change drops them all, and saved ones from another theme are not restored, since a card in another theme flashed against the live window it zooms into), so they survive a restart (an empty tab list while the store loads never prunes them): the panes on screen are captured just before any overview opens, the dragged tab's own pane included (its floating preview hides for the capture), when a window settles 900ms after a switch or layout change, when the App loses focus or hides, and every 30s while there has been no input for 5s, so a restart keeps a recent picture. Page panes use the window:capture-region IPC, which only lets a window's main page capture a region of itself and returns a JPEG at most 720px wide; web pages are captured from their own views, so off-screen ones stay current. A web view holds the page only, without the App toolbar above it, so a web pane on screen is pictured as its region with the page drawn into its frame ([data-web-frame]); the toolbar band is kept (in memory and IndexedDB) and frames pages pictured off screen, so every picture has its pane's proportions. A card is its window shrunk whole: the center area, with a lone pane flush in the center card (1rem corners) and split panes as their own cards 1px inside their slots (16px corners). Positions, insets and corners are drawn at the card's own scale, so the zoom is a plain scale and no corner or edge jumps when it lands. Pane grips are hidden for the two frames before a capture, so pictures hold only content. A pane never pictured shows only its tab's icon, centred, as Mission Control shows an app icon; its name is the card's caption, not repeated inside. As in Mission Control nothing marks the current window: opening from the button or a pinch shrinks it from where its panes sit into its card while the other cards fade in, and closing grows a card back over the center area. The zoom is a framer-motion layout animation: the zooming card is placed over the window's own place (the whole center area) and framer-motion animates it into its grid slot, or back out, over 340ms; a placeholder keeps the slot meanwhile, and every other layout change of the cards is instant. Picking a card switches to that window before the zoom, so the live window is ready underneath when the card lands; Escape and pinching out zoom the current window back; a click on empty space does nothing. Drag overviews open with a fade, since the dragged tab has left its card. A merge drop lands with one zoom, started at the release rather than after the merge commits: the commit waits on the desktop shell's transfer cancel (about 50ms) and then costs a render of the new split (about 40ms), which used to leave the dropped window gone and the card unchanged for some 90ms. The release holds the overview open, notes the drop and its tabs, and sets landing; the card it was dropped on shows that window as it is and grows from its grid place over the center area in 340ms with one uniform scale, as picking a card does, a transform animation that plays on through the wait. The window is activated under the overview as the zoom starts, as picking a card does, so its panes have mounted and loaded their content by the time they make room. commitCanvasDrop hands the merge to landOverview, which holds it and marks the landing landed. As soon as it is landed and the zoom is under way (about a tenth of a second in, after the desktop shell's transfer cancel), the overview reads where the zooming card has each pane, converts those rects into the canvas's own unscaled frame, and passes them to captureCanvasLanding together with the zoom: its start rect, its curve and how far it has got. It then closes, which applies the merge in the same tick (closing early, from a fallback timer, applies it too, without the animation). The app shell plays that capture as for any layout change: the window's panes animate their real geometry from where the card had them to their place in the split, and the docked tab comes in from the drop side at its final size, translated out and clipped at the canvas edge (clip-path), on the same 300 ms timing, so the edge between them moves as one and its content never re-wraps. Measured unscaled first, the canvas root then takes the zoom over: the same translate-and-scale keyframes as the card, with the card animation's own startTime, so both run on one clock and the curve carries on without a seam. The card zoom is given that start time explicitly when it is created: a compositor animation otherwise stays pending to the main thread while the compositor already plays it, so the canvas could not tell how far it had got, on live content rather than the pictures (small JPEGs that blurred when grown to full size). data-canvas-landing on the root keeps the centre column open page around it meanwhile, and the other cards fade in 80 ms during a landing so they are gone before the hand-over. The hint fades with them but keeps its room, so the grid does not re-centre at the release. The zoom and the split play together. No half sits still as a picture while the other changes, which reads as a stall. A merge that fails (the desktop shell keeps the tab) closes the overview at once, and the drag hint is hidden while the landing plays. In the strip a split shows where its first pane's tab sits, so dockCanvas moves the docked tabs in front of the window they join (placeDockedTabs): the split keeps that window's slot whichever side the tab went to, instead of jumping to the dragged tab's old place. A split tab names its focused pane; when that pane changes, or a merge has just formed the split, its icon and name rise in over 200ms. Once it leaves the strip, the dragged tab's slot closes by width (data-drag-out: flex-basis, width and padding to 0 and the 8px gap taken back, under the drag's own 160ms transition) so the tabs after it and the + button slide over; back over the strip it reopens. The slot stays closed through a merge drop or a move to another window, so the strip never flashes it back before the tab leaves, and reopens (with data-slot-reopen carrying the transition after the drag) only when the tab stays. The dragged tab opens into a small window that follows the pointer: its tab on top and its last picture below, 240px wide with the canvas's proportions (110 to 190px tall), opening in 200ms; back over the strip it folds into a tab again (140ms) and the real tab takes over. A tab never pictured stays a tab, and opens if a picture arrives mid-drag. A card under the pointer grows slightly. The dragged tab leaves its own card, and a card left with no tab disappears. Over a card, the left and right quarters put the tab beside the whole window and the middle puts it above or below by the half under the pointer; the card grows slightly, that half is highlighted, and a hint names the window and the side. The drop calls dockCanvas(sources, card tab, "root", side), so a tab from another window joins it and a pane dropped on its own window's card moves to that half of it. A drag keeps a gap among the cards where the tab would stand on its own (slot in the overview store; it starts where the dragged window was, or just after the window a pane is leaving), so the cards are sized for the cards plus the gap and picking one up resizes nothing. Off the cards, the gap stays while it is nearer the pointer than any card and otherwise goes beside the card nearest the pointer, on the side the pointer is; distances use where cards are laid out (offsets, without framer-motion's transforms), so a card springing aside never drags the gap after it. Over empty space the gap is outlined. A drop there, or anywhere off the cards, opens the tab on its own in the gap: the drop carries before, the card after the gap's first tab in the strip, and commitCanvasDrop moves the tab (moveTab, after ungrouping a pane from its split) or the whole window (moveGroup) there, so the strip takes the overview's order; only a pane taken out of a split is activated. The overview stays open, browsing: holdOverviewLanding turns it to browse mode and commitCanvasDrop clears sourceIds once the drop commits, so the tab's card comes back. Browsing, a card can be picked up the same way (as windows can in Mission Control): a press that moves 6px on a card turns the overview into a drag one with that window's tabs as the sources (or, on a pane of a split card, that pane's tab alone), a copy of the card at its own size follows the pointer, as a window does in Mission Control (over a card it turns see-through, so the half it would merge into shows), and the drop goes through overviewDropAt as any drag does: a card's side lands as a merge from the strip does, empty space moves the window to its gap (a pane picked out of a split gets a window of its own there), and anything but a merge browses on (browseOverview); Escape cancels. Both modes lay the cards out in the same area (the hint's room is kept, hidden, while browsing), cards are buttons in both so a switch keeps their pictures, a mode switch never replays the fade-in (data-opened-as), and empty space tints the hint rather than the whole overview. When the grid changes (a card picked up, dropped back, split or merged), the cards move and resize to their new places on a framer-motion layout spring, and a card that turns up grows in where it lands; entrances are framer-motion's too, played only when a card mounts, so a mode switch never replays them. The overview covers only the center area, so the strip still reorders, the Resources view still takes web pages, and a release beyond the window still detaches into a new one. Escape cancels, and dragging back to the strip returns to a plain reorder. The overview is a native-view occluder, so web pages hide under it. Inside a pane there are no drop zones any more: dropping onto the visible canvas used to make the bottom half and the corners hard to tell apart.

The same overview opens without a drag from the button beside the main menu in the strip, or by pinching in on the center area or the strip, as in Safari's tab overview. Clicking a card switches to that window as a tab click would; Escape, pinching out or a click outside the overview closes it; a click on the space between cards does nothing, and browsing hides the hint line but keeps its room (only a drag shows it, to name where the tab will land). Content that zooms on a pinch (the context graph) prevents the default and keeps the gesture. A web page is its own native view, so the App never sees its wheel events: a preload in the web tab session (main/web-views/webtab-preload.js, isolated world, exposes nothing) watches trusted pinch wheel events and, only when the gesture starts with the page at its normal size (visualViewport.scale 1), asks the shell to open the overview. Pinching out magnifies the page natively, as in Safari, because each web tab allows visual zoom from 1× to 3×; a pinch in on a magnified page only zooms it back, and the next pinch opens the overview.

The tiling menu mirrors the macOS green-button menu with small layout glyphs, and a hint line names the glyph under the pointer and its shortcut (otherwise it says to hold Option for quarters). Move and resize applies to this pane: left, right, top and bottom halves, replaced by the four quarters while Option is held. Fill and arrange starts with Fill. A pane sent to a half leaves the others in their order in the other half; a quarter fully held by another pane trades places. The arrangements lay out the whole canvas as left and right, left and stacked right, or quarters, with this pane first. Contents fill presets in reading order; overflow returns to the strip and spare panes stay empty. Below these are Return to previous layout, Equalize sizes, and More layouts (top and bottom, single, nine, sixteen, saved layouts and Save layout…). Closing lives on the red and yellow buttons and ⌘W.

Layout changes made from the window buttons, the tiling menu, Fill and Return to previous layout animate as macOS moves windows (lib/tabs/canvas/canvas-transition.ts). Just before the change the canvas records every pane's rect by tab id and keeps a still copy of it; the app shell plays the record in the layout effect of the commit that carried out the change, before its first frame paints. Panes are matched by tab id, so a session that moves from a peer pane to the lone chat shell still animates. The shell sits hidden during a split on whichever session was focused last; when the split dissolves it switches to the remaining session in the same tick as the layout, rather than a few renders later through the route, so its first frame already shows that session. A transcript fades in only when its rows arrive after a switch; one already loaded swaps in at once. Likewise a message row plays its appear animation only within a second of its message arriving, so the rows of a transcript that mounts again in a split pane or the shell show as they are instead of fading up from nothing. A pane that stays animates its real geometry (left, top, width, height) through the Web Animations API from the old rect to the new one in 300 ms, so text re-wraps as it grows instead of stretching, and the pane's inline calc() geometry is untouched. A pane that arrives fades in. A pane that leaves plays out as its copy: red fades and sinks (180 ms) over the panes taking its space, yellow folds into its tab in the strip as the macOS genie does (600 ms): just before the layout changes, the desktop shell takes a full-resolution still of the pane (captureRegion with full; the change waits at most 160 ms for it), and every frame draws that still on one canvas in 2 px rows, each stretched between the funnel's edges at its height, so the pane bends into a funnel whose neck is the tab's bottom edge at almost no cost per frame however heavy the pane is; without a still (a plain browser) copies of the pane are cut into horizontal bands instead, each mapped every frame onto a quad with a projective matrix3d; the edge nearest the tab pinches first, then the pane pours up through the neck, packing tighter near it, and is clipped there so it never draws over the tab; the whole tab button's background lights once as it takes the pane in, and the panes taking the space start 90 ms later; a pane leaving a split has no tab button of its own before the change, so its new button first slides out of the tab it left while the tabs after it slide over (260 ms, transform and opacity only, so the compositor plays it even while the app is still laying out the change), and the fold, aimed at where the button ends up, starts 80 ms later and reaches the strip after the button is fully out; the fold keeps its own clock, advancing at most 34 ms a frame, so a busy main thread pauses it instead of skipping part of it; in the band fallback a heavy pane gets fewer bands (6 to 20), and when its tab is not above the pane the copy shrinks toward the tab instead (340 ms), and a pane hidden by green or Fill fades under the pane that grows over it. A red close that a save prompt holds up plays when the tab is really gone; a record no commit follows lapses after 1.5 s. While the panes move, the canvas counts as dragging: web panes show their snapshot instead of the native view, and chats re-wrap only the rows in view. Pane frames and dividers fade out during the move and back in after it. Reduce Motion skips the animation. Divider drags do not animate, as their sizes already follow the pointer.

Return to previous layout first leaves Fill, then steps back past the last tile, arrange, drop, equalize, close or divider drag. Entries are found by any tab or group they involved, so a pane pulled out of the split can be put back.

Shortcuts use Control + Option because Fn + Control belongs to macOS: arrows tile the focused pane to a half; adding Shift arranges left and right or top and bottom with the focused pane on the arrow's side; F toggles Fill; R returns to the previous layout. Command/Control + Backslash splits right and adding Shift splits below. Command/Control + Shift + Enter toggles Fill. Command/Control + W closes the focused pane in a multi-pane layout. Alt + Command/Control + arrow moves spatial focus.

Drag separators to resize. Within 10px of a third, a half or two thirds of their split they stick and show the ratio. Double-click equalizes the adjacent pair; arrow keys on a focused separator move it by 2%.

A split window is one tab in the strip, as wide as any other, like Chrome's and Arc's split views. It names the pane in focus (its icon and title follow the focus), then shows the split in miniature, with that pane filled, and the number of panes. The overview shows every pane. Its close closes the whole window, saving open files first; a single pane closes from the red button on its grip. A pane closed out of a split that stays open is removed in the same tick as the click: the split's tab does not change, so there is no strip exit to wait for, and the canvas plays the close.

Drag lifecycle

The canvas will host many small app windows, so every drag must end cleanly and leave other panes alone. A pane drag's release, Escape, pointercancel, window blur and any error while resolving the drop all run one cleanup exactly once: the floating label and drop cue go, the grip returns, pointer capture is released and data-canvas-dragging is cleared. A failed drop lookup counts as no drop.

A release over the window-drag strip, a native web view or another app never reaches the page. Every drag (tab, pane, divider) registers its cancel and release with setCanvasDragging(true, owner); the first pointermove with no button down is treated as the release, and a new press while a drag is still open cancels the old one. While a drag runs, nothing in the window is a -webkit-app-region: drag region.

The dragged pane carries a data-pane-dragging attribute that hides its grip through CSS, never the grip's inline style, and a new drag clears any stale one. Pressing a grip only prepares a drag: no store commit, focus or route change, and no text selection underneath; a click focuses. Divider drags repaint at most once per frame through previewCanvas, which changes only the in-memory tree, and commit once with setCanvas on release. A native web view that must freeze is captured first and hidden when the snapshot arrives, or after 160ms, so it never flashes a paused card. The drop cue has no backdrop blur and moves with a 120ms ease-out, without overshoot.

Rendering and native views

Recursive geometry calculation feeds stable content hosts. Tree edits and zoom do not reparent content components. Zoom hides sibling panes. Tiny panes show their title and type with a zoom action. Native web views report individual bounds. During dragging, undersized presentation or beyond the eight-visible-view allowance, they show captured images. Selecting a paused view gives it priority. Hidden native views remain alive; this is not process termination.

Persistence and boundaries

The existing window tab payload stores each group's canvas tree, focus and zoom. Missing content references become empty panes. Cross-window payload validation bounds tree depth, node count, and serialized size independently of the normal layout editor. Resources attachment continues to use its existing backend ownership operation.

Implementation status

The tree, store actions, canvas controls, drop presentation, snapshot presentation and persistence are implemented, including macOS-style edge and corner tiling, the hover tiling menu, Control + Option shortcuts, divider snapping and the one-tab split window in the strip. The interactive prototype linked from the Chinese specification still demonstrates the earlier centre replace and toolbar; the text is authoritative.

Dragging a pane into the top tab strip shows the same blue rounded drop preview as canvas docking. The cue follows the actual tab-strip bounds and disappears when leaving or cancelling; releasing inside those bounds returns the pane to a standalone tab.
