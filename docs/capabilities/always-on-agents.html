Always-on Agents

Each conversation with an always-on Agent wakes when something it watches is due, works on a standing goal, checks for news with read-only tools when idle, and contacts you only when something deserves it.

Always-on is part of an Agent's configuration. Every conversation with that Agent is its own always-on assistant, with its own Goal, check-ins and reports. For two separate assistants, start two conversations.

Turn it on

Butler and the built-in job Agents have it on: start a conversation with one, from the Agent chip in a composer or its card on the Agents page, and say what to watch or continue. For your own Agent:

  1. Open Agents in the sidebar and select it.
  2. In the Always-on tab check Keep conversations with this Agent always on.
  3. Optional: a Standing goal, a fixed wake interval, and under When it wakes the wake days and Quiet hours.
  4. Save changes.
  5. Talk to it: New conversation on the Agents page, or the Agent chip.
SettingValue
Standing goalSomething to achieve ("Get the docs site free of broken links"), not a routine ("Check the links every hour"). The Goal ends once its requirements hold; looking again later is what check-ins do.
Fixed wake interval5 to 10080 minutes. Empty: the agent schedules its own wakes. With check-ins off, it cannot schedule a check-in. Try chats of a draft never wake.
Wake onWeekdays scheduled wakes happen on. At least one stays on.
Quiet hoursDaily From and Until, such as 22:00 to 08:00. An end before the start runs past midnight.

Wake days and quiet hours use this computer's clock and time zone. By default a conversation may wake at any time. Wake now, messages from your other conversations and a running Goal still run during quiet time.

A scheduled check-in is skipped without a model call when there is no standing goal, no unfinished open work or todos, and no new message since the last check-in. A check-in that failed or was cut off by a restart before it reported does not count as the last one.

A conversation you have not written in is never checked. Self-scheduled check-ins and Wake now always run. A goal changed while another is unfinished starts when that one ends; /goal clear switches at once.

For a fixed schedule ("every day at 9, summarize my inbox"), ask for a scheduled task instead.

Scheduled tasks
  • Run in the background, outside your branch list. Each run starts clean: it sees the task and its last result, not the conversation. Nothing new: you hear nothing. Something changed: an ordinary message in the conversation, with a notification.
  • Three failures in a row: it tells you once.
  • The Activity panel shows each task's last result and last 20 runs; each opens in place to show what it did. The Agent can see the task and read its results.

Check-ins

A check-in reviews memory, the conversation's open work and the conversation for open threads, and looks for anything new. Nothing of it appears in the conversation: you can keep typing, and its place in the sidebar does not change. However long the conversation is, a check-in reads its summary and latest part, looks further back only when it needs to, and never compacts it.

Each check-in sees earlier reports and repeats them only if something changed; an Agent with picked channels also sees report titles from its other conversations. Pages, files and earlier reports are data: it never follows instructions in them.

Reports

KindMeans
findingSomething you may want to know
approvalYou need to decide something
doneA piece of work finished

A report has a title, a body of one to three sentences and optional links, and arrives as a message from the Agent, so your next message can answer it. If a turn is running, it follows that turn. A check-in with nothing to report leaves no trace in the conversation; the Activity panel still shows its result.

In the App the message is the body and links, with web links shown by site name; the title stays in lists and notifications. In the chat view, a time line above it says why it came: noticed while checking in, finished in the background, asks for your decision, or the name of a scheduled task.

Tell it what to report in the conversation ("don't tell me about passing builds"). Each rule becomes a report preference of the Agent, followed by every later check-in and scheduled run. Only your own turns can add one, never a check-in or scheduled run. The Activity panel lists them for deletion, and each report offers to stop reports like it.

Send reports to channels
  • Under Also send reports to, check up to 10 conversations on a chat channel: Telegram, Slack, Discord or WeChat. They apply to reports from every conversation with this Agent. A chat bound to a channel gets its own reports there without being checked, once; the Channels setting turns both off.
  • The list holds the Agent's existing channel conversations plus people and groups bound with a peer binding: openprogram channels bindings add <agent> --channel telegram --account <id> --peer <id>. An Agent bound to a whole channel or account lists only people who have talked to it.
  • Each report is sent once per picked conversation, after it is saved in the App. The Activity panel shows the outcome under each report; a failed send, such as a signed-out account, is shown there and the report is kept.
  • When that person or group already has its own conversation, the report also appears there as a message, so a reply on the phone has its context, and it answers a report waiting for a decision.

Hand work to another conversation

Conversations can work as a team, one owner per job. An always-on conversation's agent has a handoff tool that messages another of your conversations by title or ID, or by an Agent's name when that Agent has one other conversation; the other may use any Agent. Give each conversation a clear title and job, and tell the planning one which does what.

StepBehaviour
DeliveryArrives as the next turn, starting with 📨 and the sender's title. Waits for a running turn, up to ten minutes.
ReplyWhen that turn really ends its answer returns as a new turn starting with ↩️, also after it stopped for your approval. If the turn failed or was stopped, the sender is told that instead, with the reason. If the other stayed busy or was archived meanwhile, the message comes back undelivered.
Passed onIf the receiver hands the work to a third conversation, the first sender waits for the receiver's answer after the third has replied, not for its "I asked them".
Chat appsThe answer to a 📨 goes only to the sender, not to the receiving conversation's chat app. The ↩️ turn's answer does go to the sender's chat app.
PermissionsEach turn runs under its own conversation's Agent, mode and rules. In Manual, sending waits for approval; in Auto it is reviewed.
RefusedMessaging itself; messaging a conversation waiting further up the chain; more than four hops in a chain, also through a Goal the chain started; more than 20 messages an hour from one conversation or in one chain; sub-agent, imported or archived conversations as sender or receiver
RestartChains survive a service restart for a day: the hop count holds, and a message still waiting for a busy conversation is delivered once, a conversation working on a message still answers when it finishes, and if the restart ends that work, the sender is told

In the Activity panel

Activity panel line

In the conversation's Activity panel the main agent's row gets an Always-on line: whether a check-in is running and the next wake after quiet hours, with the agent's reason when it chose that wake.

Its page has an Always-on row above the runs: standing goal, current Goal, wake schedule, whether it is alive (when it last woke, wakes and failures in 24 hours, or a wake long overdue), last wake with result and cost, today's check-in spending and report preferences. Reports are rows among the runs.

Wake now runs a wake now, or in five minutes if a turn is running, and retries a check-in that failed or was interrupted by a restart. A later check-in the Agent set for itself stays as it was.

A new, empty chat with an always-on Agent offers three things to say to it. Clicking one only fills the box; it disappears once the box has text.

A conversation with an always-on Agent reads like a chat with a bot: short paragraphs as separate bubbles (at most four, and code, tables and lists stay with the text around them), the time shown again after 30 minutes and on a new day. Thinking, tool steps and message buttons are not drawn; three dots show while the Agent works after its last words.

A reply that fails or stops short says so in a line under it, a reply cut by a restart continues as the one reply, and web sources sit once under the last bubble. Nothing is removed; this also applies to older conversations, and turning always-on off brings the full view back.

In Telegram, Discord, Slack and WeChat the Agent writes shorter, offers choices as a numbered list, and a table arrives as one line per row, sent only once it is complete.

Limits

TopicLimit
Where it runsWork runs on this computer, in the conversation's folder and sandbox. The worker must be running.
StoppingTurning always-on off or archiving a conversation stops its wakes. Conversations, reports, memory and the settings stay.
What it sendsOutgoing messages through connected services follow the conversation's permission mode. To review each first, turn on Hold what it sends for my approval at the end of the Always-on tab. A check-in has no one to ask, so a held message comes back as a draft in its report.
A copy of the AgentA duplicated Agent starts with always-on off.
Time limitA check-in stops after 15 minutes, and the last wake says so. Wake now is the retry.
Reports and restartsA report waiting for a running turn is added after that turn. After a service restart, reports under an hour old are still added and their chat-app sends resumed, so a chat app may rarely get one twice; older ones stay in the Activity panel, and an unfinished send shows as cut off by a restart.
Your browserA check-in, scheduled run, automatic Goal turn, handoff or chat-app message, and anything it starts, never takes the browser page you have in front. It opens its own tab behind your work, or uses a page it names from the page list.
LearningIt learns through memory: set Read and write in the Agent's Memory tab so corrections are saved and used. The Always-on tab reminds you otherwise. Your replies to a report count; the report itself does not. A scheduled run counts only if you continue its branch.
